Interactive explainer: On-center and off-center neurons in the lateral geniculate nucleus — explore how excitation and inhibition from cones, bipolar cells, and horizontal cells shape center-surround receptive fields.

Your retina doesn't send raw pixel values to the brain. Before the signal ever leaves your eye, neurons perform a computation: they compare what's at a spot against what surrounds it. This is the center-surround receptive field.

Firing
The center cone drives the bipolar cell directly. Adjust the spot to illuminate surround cones — horizontal cells will pull the response down.

The key tension: the center cone sends a direct excitatory signal to the bipolar cell. Surround cones activate the horizontal cell, which feeds laterally inhibitory input back onto the bipolar cell's dendrites — competing with the center signal. In an on-center cell, light in the center excites and light in the surround suppresses.
